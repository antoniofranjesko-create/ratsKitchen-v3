Your turn
  1. Rat King check: 10+ rat weight = you win.
  2. Cards on your rats or kitchen resolve (Hot Chilli goes off).
  3. Draw 1 market card.
  4. One choice: play up to 2 cards (1 Attack + 1 Defend) · or take the top rat · or draw another card · or take 1 Food.
  5. End: a Territorial on you ends.
Free on your turn: fire your armed inspection; feed rats (each once). Heal: 3 Food = 1 star (your Defend card).
Three of a kind: first time you hold 3 rats of one breed, take 1 Food, then turn this card over (once per game).

Rat's Kitchen · Long mode rules

A 30-minute game for 3–6 players. You run a stall in the night market, build up your rats, and bring the Health Inspector down on everyone else.

Winning

Rat King: start your own turn with 10 or more rat weight in your kitchen. Reaching 10 isn't enough on its own: you must still hold it when your next turn begins, so everyone gets one round to knock you down.
Last stall standing: every other stall has been shut down.

Components

Market deck: 81 cards + 4 Hot Ratato · Rat deck: 37 rats · Market Day: 6 cards + 1 spare Mole · Food pile: 27 Food cards · 6 reference cards. Also needed: star tokens (coins or beads; up to 7 per player) and a first-player marker.

Setup

  1. Shuffle the market deck and the rat deck separately, side by side.
  2. Each player takes stars: 3 players 7 · 4 players 6 · 5 players 5 · 6 players 4.
  3. Each player takes a reference card and 2 Food.
  4. Deal each player 6 market cards. Any Snap Inspection or Hot Ratato dealt is set aside and shuffled back in afterwards.
  5. Put the Market Day cards in a face-up pile, the spare Mole beside it, and the Food pile in the middle.
  6. Give the starting player the first-player marker. Play passes left.

The table

  • Your kitchen (your rats and anything on them) is public. Your hand is secret. There is no hand limit.
  • Breeds: every rat is a Rex ➰, Hairless 🥚 or Dumbo 👂. Sewer Rats have no breed. Inspections check one breed.
  • Rat deck: face-down. Any rat leaving play is shuffled back into it, and anything on it is discarded.
  • Closed stalls: a shut-down stall's hand stays on the table as a face-down pile.
  • Food is a card in your hand. Spent Food goes back to the Food pile.
  • Touch rule: you can affect each rival's kitchen only once per turn.

Your turn

  1. Rat King check: 10+ rat weight now, you win.
  2. Resolve cards on your rats or kitchen: a Hot Chilli on your rat goes off (the rat goes back into the rat deck).
  3. Draw 1 market card. A Snap Inspection fires on you straight away.
  4. Make one choice:
    • Play cards: up to 2, 1 Attack (aimed at a rival: their kitchen, rats or hand) and 1 Defend (on your own kitchen). Arming a Scheduled Inspection is your Attack. Special Delivery counts as whichever kitchen the rat goes to. The table can agree 1 card instead.
    • Take a rat: the top rat of the rat deck goes into your kitchen.
    • Draw another market card.
    • Take 1 Food from the Food pile.
  5. End: a Territorial on your kitchen ends; discard it.

Free at any point in your turn: fire an inspection you armed last turn; feed rats (each rat once per turn). Healing (3 Food for 1 star) counts as your Defend card.

Reactions are played free on someone else's turn: Grease the Palm cancels an inspection aimed at you; Wok Block cancels any other attack aimed at you.

Three of a kind: the first time you hold 3 rats of the same breed, take 1 Food, then turn your reference card over. Once per game.

A round is one pass round the table. At the start of each round, flip the next Market Day; it lasts that round.

Rats

Every rat has a breed, a weight (counts towards Rat King), a heat (damage when its breed is inspected) and Teeth (for Scrap Fights; every rat has a different number, so fights never tie). Some have an ability.
Feeding: pay the Food shown to use a Feed ability. Each rat can be fed once per turn: turn it sideways, and straighten it at the start of your next turn.

Inspections

An inspection names a breed. The target loses stars equal to the total heat of their rats of that breed; no rats of that breed, nothing happens. Rats are never removed by an inspection.

  • Scheduled Inspection: arm it in front of you (your Attack). On your next turn fire it at a rival for free; if you don't, it expires at the end of that turn. Two-breed cards: pick one breed when you fire. Only one armed at a time.
  • Health Inspection: a rival loses 1 star, whatever their rats.
  • Snap Inspections fire on you the moment you draw them.
  • You can't fire at a boarded-up kitchen, or one you've already touched this turn.

Card types

The band at the top of each card says how it's played: Attack (aimed at a rival), Defend (on your own kitchen), Attack or Defend (by where it goes), Reaction (free, on others' turns), Fires when drawn. Each card's own text gives its effect.

Scrap Fight

  1. Challenge a rival who has a rat; you need one too.
  2. Both secretly choose one of your own rats and reveal together.
  3. Longer Teeth wins. The winning rat grabs the scrap and bolts: it goes back into the rat deck.
  4. If you (the challenger) win, your rival loses 1 star. If they win, nobody loses a star.

Sending a heavy rat to win costs you Rat King weight, so bluffing pays both ways.

Shutting down

Lose your last star and your stall is shut down. Your rats go back into the rat deck. If someone placed Inheritance on you, they take 2 of your cards; the rest of your hand stays on the table face-down as a closed stall (Scavenger Rats can take from it).

Rats can't be killed. They only ever move somewhere else.
